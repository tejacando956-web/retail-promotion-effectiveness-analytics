How the numbers are built. Two layers sit behind this dashboard, and the filters re-add both. Daily measures (revenue, units, margin %, discount cost) are summed from a daily cube — sales aggregated by date × store × category × discount level × campaign — over the rows the filters select. Campaign measures (uplift, incremental revenue and gross profit, ROI, dip, margins, profitable count) are re-added from the campaign results in scope: the published campaign rows from outputs/campaign_performance.csv, or — when stores are filtered — from a campaign × store table computed with the same campaign-window method run inside each store (each store judged against its own 28 clean baseline days, exactly like outputs/promo_by_store.csv). Uplift and the margins are weighted by units sold on promotion; dip is the simple average over the campaigns (or campaign × store cells) in scope; ROI is incremental gross profit ÷ discount cost over the scope. The default view — every filter on “All” — reproduces the project's 15 headline KPIs exactly.

Method in one line: each campaign is compared with the 28 clean days (no promotion, no dip week) before it, per SKU-store-day; the first two campaigns use all the clean days available (12 and 19). Synthetic data, generated with a fixed seed — a portfolio work sample, not real company data. Full method, formulas and code: README.md, KPI_DICTIONARY.md and analysis.py in this repo.
